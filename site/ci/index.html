How long a pull request waits

Per head commit of a pull request, the longest job of its CI, Simulator CI and nightly dry-run runs, which is what the author waits for; a re-run counts the whole time from its first start. The second line is the same for commits pushed to main. Queueing is not in GitHub's job timings, so real waits are a little longer.

Local builds, measured daily on a GitHub macOS runner at the tip of main (make build-bench): for the trend, not for the seconds on your Mac. A point is the median of that day's runs, the band is fastest to slowest, and an orange dot on the axis marks a day the runner reported load: read it as noise.

CI: every job

One line per job that finished green on main, taken from the data as it comes: a job that is new starts a new line, a job that was removed ends its line, and a run without a job leaves a gap, not a zero.

Simulator CI: every job

The same, for the simulator workflow.

Runner minutes per push

The sum of every green job's minutes, which several optimisations traded wall time against: a split into parallel jobs lowers the wait and raises this. The total counts only commits that ran both workflows.

Red pushes and re-runs, per week

The share of finished CI and Simulator CI runs on main that failed, and that passed only after a re-run. Cancelled runs (a newer push superseding an older one) are not timed or counted in the share; the tooltip says how many there were.

Host checks ran as one job until Oct 1; since then they are parallel jobs plus a short aggregator, and the chart shows each separately. Runner timings on shared GitHub machines are noisy, so read the median, not single runs. The thresholds that decide worse, better and flat are in tools/ci-summary.py.

The data is ci-history.json, build-history.json and ci-summary.json on the ci-metrics branch. Runs older than 90 days are folded into one row per day.
