# v0.5.0 — Auto

This release is for the link you actually have. Until now the app asked your server for one
quality and lived with the consequences: pick Original on a connection that cannot carry it and you
got a film that stalled. **Auto** is a new entry in the playback menu's Quality section that
measures the connection while you watch and moves between qualities as it changes. It is not on by
default — Original still is, and you choose Auto per playback.

## Which televisions this works on

- **Plays video — 2019 LG sets.** Verified by watching it, on one television: an LG 49SM9000PLA,
  which reports platform release 4.10.2 (webosbrew's `goldilocks2` bucket; LG markets this
  generation as webOS 4.5). A 27-case suite runs on that set against a live Plex Media Server
  before every release, and a second 19-case suite exercises Auto against a deliberately throttled
  connection.
- **Plays video — one 2021 LG set, reported.** @mariotaku of webosbrew ran v0.2.1 on an LG
  65UP7560AUD (platform release 6.5.2) on 2026-08-11 and got a picture:
  [issue #22](https://github.com/GLinnik21/plx-native/issues/22). Six of his eight attempts played
  — H.264 at 1080p and 4K, HEVC, AAC and AC3, an episode, and an item watched to the end. The two
  that failed were a server-side defect v0.3.0 fixed, and neither was about the television.
  That is one firmware, one set, one report, and it remains the only time video has been seen on
  anything but the developer's own television.
- **Starts — and nothing further is known — every other firmware from webOS 4.0 up.** The loader
  resolves this binary's libraries and symbols cleanly against all nine firmware images webosbrew
  has at 4.0 or newer, webOS 4.4.2 through 11.2.0. That is a static check against LG's own symbol
  tables: it says the process starts, and says nothing at all about whether video plays.
- **Does not start — webOS 3.9.2 and older.** Symbols the app needs are missing from those
  firmwares, so the loader kills the process before anything appears. Installing it there gets you
  a tile that does nothing.

If your set is in the middle two groups, tell us what happened —
[open an issue](https://github.com/GLinnik21/plx-native/issues). It working is as useful a report
as it failing.

## Help wanted

**If your connection to your server is slow, congested or over Wi-Fi, try Auto and say what you
saw.** Everything below was measured on one television against one server, with the connection
throttled deliberately — which is a laboratory, not a house. What nobody here can reproduce is a
real link: a neighbour's microwave, a server that is also transcoding for somebody else, a mesh
node three rooms away. Whether the picture settled, how long it took, and whether it ever went
backwards are all useful.
[Open an issue](https://github.com/GLinnik21/plx-native/issues). It working is as useful a report
as it failing.

**If a friend has shared a library with you, try it.** Unchanged from v0.4.0 and still true: a
share reached at a public address, one that requires an encrypted connection, one behind Plex's
relay, and an account with several shares at once are all paths nobody here can reproduce.

## Auto

Open the playback menu, choose **Auto** under Quality, and the app takes over the decision. It
starts at a quality it has evidence the connection can carry, watches how long each piece of video
actually takes to arrive, and moves up or down as that changes. Moving up is a background
operation: it fetches a sample at the higher quality first and only switches if that sample
arrives in time, so a quality it cannot sustain is refused before you see it rather than after.

Two things about it are worth knowing because they are visible.

**It prefers your original file and goes back to it.** Auto is not a bitrate ladder with Original
bolted on top. If the connection recovers enough to carry the file as it sits on your server, Auto
returns to it — no re-encoding, the original picture and the original soundtrack, Dolby Vision and
Atmos included. It checks for that opportunity periodically and only takes it when the reserve it
has built up can pay for the check, so a marginal connection is not repeatedly interrupted by
attempts to leave it.

**It will not move for a change you cannot see.** Eight of the qualities it can choose between are
the same frame size, so most of its decisions change nothing visible; the ones that do cross a
size boundary are treated as a different class of event. A switch also costs something — the
picture is rebuilt — so a small gain late in a film is declined outright rather than paid for.

Original and the five fixed qualities are unchanged. If you never open that menu, nothing about
this release changes what your television does.

## Fixed

- **A quality change no longer stalls and then races.** After switching down, playback could stop
  for a second, sprint through what it had missed, stop again, and repeat — sometimes for a
  minute. The cause was a switch that spent the entire remaining buffer on its own transfer and
  then handed the player an empty queue, so the film ran a segment at a time straight off the
  network. A switch now works to a deadline: it stops and stays where it is rather than
  overrunning, and what it spends is bounded by what is left to play. On the case that reproduced
  it, the longest stall went from 8 seconds to none, and the stutter from 14 affected seconds to 1.
- **A failed transfer can no longer make the connection look faster than it is.** An abandoned
  fetch was being folded into the measurement as though it had completed, so the estimate rose at
  the exact moment the link had collapsed, and the next decision was made on a number that could be
  an order of magnitude too high.

## Installing

Download **com.beb.plxnative_0.5.0_arm.ipk** — that is the app. The other four files are the
Homebrew Channel's manifest, the checksum, and the FFmpeg source we are obliged to publish.

Install it with the [Homebrew Channel](https://github.com/webosbrew/webos-homebrew-channel) or
[dev-manager-desktop](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop). **Prefer the Homebrew
Channel:** LG expires a Developer Mode session after about 1000 hours and *uninstalls the apps
installed through it* when it does. You do **not** need a rooted TV — the app runs in LG's normal
sandbox, unprivileged, with no special permissions.

## Checking what you downloaded

Nothing anywhere in this distribution chain is signed — there is no code signing in the webosbrew
path at all — so this sha256 is what tells you the file you have is the file that was published
here.

```
__IPK_SHA256__  com.beb.plxnative_0.5.0_arm.ipk
```

Check it with `shasum -a 256 com.beb.plxnative_0.5.0_arm.ipk` on macOS or Linux, or
`certutil -hashfile com.beb.plxnative_0.5.0_arm.ipk SHA256` on Windows. With the `ipk.sha256`
asset beside the file, `sha256sum -c ipk.sha256` does the same in one step.

If the Homebrew Channel installs this for you from its catalogue, it fetches this release's
`com.beb.plxnative.manifest.json`, hashes the download on the television and refuses to install a
package that does not match — you have nothing to do. Pointing the Channel at a bare `.ipk`
yourself skips that check, so check it yourself.

Built by GitHub Actions from commit `__COMMIT__`, [run __RUN_URL__]. The assets attached here are
that run's artifacts, unmodified — nothing was rebuilt or re-uploaded by hand.

Two builds of this commit on one machine produce a byte-identical `.ipk`. It is **not** reproducible
across machines yet — the bundled FFmpeg records the directory it was built in — so a hash from
your own rebuild will differ, and that is not tampering.

## Source for the bundled FFmpeg

This package contains three FFmpeg shared libraries — `libavformat-plx.so.63`,
`libavcodec-plx.so.63` and `libavutil-plx.so.61` — built from **FFmpeg 9.0**, unmodified, and
licensed **LGPL-2.1-or-later**. Demuxers, parsers, bitstream filters and subtitle decoders only:
video and audio are decoded by the television's own hardware.

The complete corresponding source is attached to this release:

- `ffmpeg-9.0.tar.xz` — the pristine upstream tarball, sha256
  `7f607a00dd0d28a729d5a4811205812eef01cf6ef6155025febb6f36a9062d52`, from
  <https://ffmpeg.org/releases/>. No patches are applied.
- `build-ffmpeg.sh` — the complete configure invocation that produced the libraries. Built with
  `--disable-everything` plus an explicit component list, and **without** `--enable-gpl`,
  `--enable-version3` or `--enable-nonfree`, so no GPL or non-free component is present.

They are ordinary shared libraries, `dlopen`ed by absolute path from the app's own directory under
exactly those names — so they can neither shadow nor be shadowed by the television's own FFmpeg,
and a build of your own with the same names replaces ours. Full text in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`
and `licenses/LGPL-2.1.txt`, both inside the package.

## Still the same scope

Movies and TV shows, from a Plex Media Server your account can reach. No music, no photos, no live
TV or DVR, and no way to type in a server address — the app connects to a server your Plex account
already knows about, or to nothing.

## Known not to work

- **Every server transcode, on platform release 10.3.1** — which means Auto and the five fixed
  qualities, leaving only Original. Measured on a rented set on 2026-08-27: the app declares its
  video sink at the panel maximum whatever the codec, and that television refuses an H.264 stream
  at those dimensions with a resource-allocation error before a frame is decoded. HEVC at the same
  ceiling plays, so direct play of an HEVC file is unaffected. Not fixed in this release, and
  `docs/webos10-resource-allocation.md` is the full account including the one-line change that was
  proven and deliberately not taken.
- Auto has been exercised against a throttled connection on one television and one server. The
  behaviours it is designed for — a link that degrades slowly, a server already busy transcoding
  for somebody else — have not been observed anywhere but in that laboratory.
- Playback on webOS 5, 7, 8, 9 and 11 remains unverified: the app starts there and nobody has
  reported watching anything.
- Remote-only servers behind Plex's relay, and servers that require an encrypted connection, are
  reached but have not been watched end to end.
- The app tells your server it can decode HEVC, 4K and 10-bit by reading the television's own codec
  table, but ignores the frame-rate limit in that same table — so a set that decodes 4K only at 30
  frames per second is told nothing about it.
